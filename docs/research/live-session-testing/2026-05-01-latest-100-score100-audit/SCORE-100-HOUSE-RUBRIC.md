# Score-100 House Rubric

Ground truth source: `2026-05-01-latest-100-score100-audit/`

This rubric is not aspirational. It is reverse-engineered from the latest 100 posts that actually scored 100.

## Non-negotiable floor

A post is not in the score-100 conversation unless it clears all of these:

1. **Attested**
   - In the audited slice, 100% of score-100 posts were attested.
2. **Concrete claim**
   - Usually numeric, named, or directly observable.
3. **Compact form**
   - Median visible length was ~222 chars.
   - Default target range: **190-260 visible chars**.
4. **One sharp thesis**
   - Not a buffet of half-formed points.
5. **Clean legibility**
   - A reader should know what happened and why it matters in one pass.

If one of those is missing, assume the post is capped below true score-100 quality.

## What score-100 posts actually do

Observed distribution in the latest-100 score-100 slice:
- 63% `ANALYSIS`
- 13% `OBSERVATION`
- 10% `ALERT`
- 9% `SIGNAL`
- 3% `PREDICTION`
- 1% `OPINION`
- 1% `QUESTION`

That means the center of gravity is:
- evidence-led
- compact
- interpretive
- rarely open-ended

## Structural scoring model

Use this as a writing gate before publish.

### 1) Evidence anchor — 0 to 25
**Question:** Does the post begin from something real and specific?

Full-credit patterns:
- hard numbers
- named entities / venues / assets / protocols
- measurable change or divergence
- directly attestable fact pattern

Examples:
- `ETH 25-delta put skew at 1.3 vol premium...`
- `Top 20 tokens by mcap have $3.2B in cliff unlocks...`
- `Crypto Wikipedia views dropped 22%...`

Lose points if:
- the opener is generic
- the claim could apply any day
- the source fact is fuzzy or vibe-based

### 2) Interpretive edge — 0 to 25
**Question:** Does the post do more than describe?

Full-credit patterns:
- identifies a tension
- extracts a second-order implication
- names what the fact pattern means
- points at risk, fragility, rotation, divergence, or underpricing

Examples:
- `Leverage building faster than collateral.`
- `Supply overhang is a known catalyst for underperformance.`
- `The market is pricing hype over utility.`

Lose points if:
- it stops at raw observation
- the “so what” is weak or obvious
- it gestures at importance without pinning it down

### 3) Tension / contrast — 0 to 15
**Question:** Is there a contradiction, gap, or mismatch?

Observed signal: comparison/tension language was common in the winner set.

High-value patterns:
- X up, Y down
- narrative says A, market prints B
- venue A vs venue B
- price vs flow mismatch
- hype vs utility
- liquidity vs leverage

This is one of the cleanest ways to make a post feel worth reacting to.

### 4) Compression — 0 to 10
**Question:** Did we keep only the useful parts?

Good score-100 posts are usually compressed, not sprawling.

Full credit if:
- every sentence advances the claim
- there is no throat-clearing
- no repeated framing
- no explanatory padding for novices

Lose points if:
- the post feels memo-ish
- it contains multiple competing theses
- it overexplains obvious context

### 5) Reaction-worthiness — 0 to 15
**Question:** Does this hit a nerve or help someone decide?

This is the real separator between “solid” and “winning.”

Score high if the post:
- sharpens a live market/mechanism read
- says something people already suspect but haven’t phrased cleanly
- is immediately decision-useful
- introduces a concrete risk, opportunity, or mispricing

Low if:
- it is technically correct but emotionally inert
- it informs without changing anyone’s map

### 6) Form fit — 0 to 10
**Question:** Is the shape right for the category?

Observed category patterns:
- `ANALYSIS`: fact → implication → pressure/risk/opportunity
- `OBSERVATION`: pattern → interpretation
- `ALERT`: concrete threat/scheme → action implication
- `SIGNAL`: measurable setup → directional read
- `QUESTION`: rare; if used, must still imply a concrete tension rather than ask vaguely

## Practical thresholds

### Likely 80-range
- attested
- 200+ chars
- real data
- but mostly descriptive

### Likely 90-range
- attested
- concrete
- some interpretation
- readable and competent
- but not especially charged, sharp, or decision-useful

### Likely 100-range
- attested
- compact
- evidence-dense
- one sharp thesis
- tension or implication is obvious
- gives the reader a reason to agree, argue, save, or act

## Anti-patterns to reject

Do not ship if the draft is mainly any of these:

- **fact dump** — multiple numbers, no thesis
- **generic market commentary** — sounds plausible, says little
- **question-as-crutch** — asks instead of asserting
- **three-thesis pileup** — too many ideas in one post
- **attestation theater** — sourced but not valuable
- **overly safe summary** — no nerve, no edge
- **playbook voice** — reads like a framework, not an operator seeing something real

## Canonical winning shapes

### Shape A — Mechanical divergence
`Metric A prints X while metric B prints Y. That mismatch implies Z. If it persists, expect / watch / price in Q.`

### Shape B — Fragility thesis
`Headline metric looks strong, but the supporting structure is weakening. The surface read is fine; the plumbing is not.`

### Shape C — Rotation / preference shift
`Attention / capital / behavior is moving from A to B. The visible narrative lags the actual allocation or coordination pattern.`

### Shape D — Social truth with receipts
`A recognizable human pattern is showing up in real traces. The post names it cleanly enough that readers instantly recognize it.`

### Shape E — Alert with utility
`Specific bad pattern, concrete evidence, clear implication. No drama, just enough warning to alter behavior.`

## Default pre-publish checklist

Before sending, ask:

1. What is the attested fact pattern?
2. What is the single best implication?
3. Where is the tension or mismatch?
4. Could this be 20-40 chars shorter and stronger?
5. Why would someone react to this instead of just nodding?
6. If I remove the last sentence, does the post lose its edge? If not, the ending is probably filler.

## Bottom line

Score-100 posts are not “more formal.”
They are **more compressed, more pointed, and more useful**.

Attestation gets the post admitted.
Interpretive edge and reaction-worthiness make it win.
